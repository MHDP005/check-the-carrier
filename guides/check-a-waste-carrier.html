How to check a waste carrier is licensed

If you use a skip company, skip bag collection or house clearance service in England, GOV.UK says you must check they are on the register of waste carriers, and that you should select “upper tier”.

Official register (England)

Search the Environment Agency’s Register of Waste Carriers, Brokers and Dealers. You can search by business name, registration number or postcode. The Environment Agency enquiry line is 03708 506 506, Monday to Friday, 8am to 6pm.

Why this check is your legal duty

As the occupier of a domestic property, you have a duty under section 34(2A) of the Environmental Protection Act 1990 to take all reasonable measures available to you to make sure you only transfer household waste from that property to an authorised person. This is explained in section 5 of the Waste Duty of Care Code of Practice.

GOV.UK is clear: you could be fined if you give your household waste to someone who is not legally allowed to take it. That can happen, for example, if fly-tipped waste is later identified as yours.

What you could face

Your council may give you a fixed penalty notice, or you could be prosecuted and face a fine and a criminal record. GOV.UK’s guidance for local authorities, updated on 11 September 2023, states that the maximum full penalty for breaches of the household waste duty of care is now £600. Paying a fixed penalty within the specified period discharges liability for that offence, but you do not have to accept the offer of a fixed penalty.

An authorised carrier can still fly-tip. Official guidance says tracing waste back to a household does not automatically prove a breach. You should be given a chance to show that you took reasonable steps to check the person was authorised.

How to do the check

  1. Ask for the registration number before you book. The Code of Practice says each carrier should have a number starting with CBDU, followed by digits. Ask for the legal business name as well as any trading name.
  2. Open the official English register at environment.data.gov.uk/public-register/view/search-waste-carriers-brokers. If the work is in Wales, Scotland or Northern Ireland, use that nation’s register instead (links below).
  3. Select upper tier for a paid skip, skip bag or clearance company. GOV.UK says: “If you use a private business that provides a waste collection service, you must check the company is on the register of waste carriers. You need to select ‘upper tier’.” Lower tier is mainly for charities, voluntary organisations, councils, or businesses only carrying their own waste (with limited exceptions).
  4. Match the details. Check the name, registration type (upper tier), status and expiry date. The Code of Practice says an upper-tier registration allows a person or organisation to transport other people’s waste.
  5. If you are taking waste to a private site yourself, also check the site has an environmental permit or a registered waste exemption, using the Environment Agency’s public registers for those, or ask the Environment Agency or your council to help you check.

Upper tier and lower tier, in brief

The Environment Agency register explains the difference. A business will usually need an upper-tier registration if it transports other people’s waste (a typical skip company), carries construction and demolition waste, arranges for other businesses’ waste to be transported, or buys and sells waste. Lower-tier examples include charities and voluntary organisations, and some businesses that only carry their own waste (not construction or demolition waste).

If you arrange for a charity to collect items, GOV.UK says you must still check they are on the register, and that you should select “lower tier”.

Waste transfer notes — what householders need to know

A waste transfer note is the written record used when non-hazardous waste is transferred from one business to another. For business waste, GOV.UK says both sides must complete a waste transfer note or a document with the same information (an invoice can be enough), sign it, and keep a copy for two years. There is an official duty of care waste transfer note form.

Householders are treated differently. Section 5.1 of the Code of Practice says the wider duty of care, including the requirement for waste transfer notes, “specifically does not apply to the occupier of a domestic property when they are dealing with their household waste.” Guidance for local authorities on household duty-of-care fixed penalties says the same: there is no duty on an individual to keep a waste transfer note or any other written records when transferring household waste.

That does not mean paperwork is useless. Section 5.6 of the Code of Practice says there is no legal requirement to keep records of your checks, but if your waste is later fly-tipped, records can quickly show that you met your duty of care. It suggests you could:

  • record any checks you make, including the operator’s registration, permit or exemption number
  • keep a receipt that includes the business details of a registered operator
  • ask for a copy, or take a photograph, of the carrier’s waste registration or the site’s permit
  • record details of the business or of any vehicle used (registration, make, model, colour) that can be linked back to an authorised operator

It is still reasonable to ask the company what will happen to your waste, and to ask for a receipt or a copy of any transfer documentation they complete. A legitimate carrier should not object to those questions.

If you hire a tradesperson

GOV.UK says that if you hire a tradesperson for construction or renovation work, any waste they produce is classed as business waste. The tradesperson is responsible for removing it and disposing of it correctly. The Code of Practice adds that if you have a tradesperson take away waste that you produced — for example if you add your own waste to their skip — your duty of care applies to that waste.

If your waste is later fly-tipped

England recorded 1.26 million fly-tipping incidents in 2024/25, and 62% involved household waste. Keep your records. Official guidance says you should be given a chance to show you took reasonable steps. If you cannot identify who took the waste, or the carrier you name is unauthorised, the guidance says it is reasonable to believe the duty of care was not met.

Registers outside England

Carriers registered in Scotland or Wales are also authorised to operate in England, according to the Environment Agency register, but you should still use the right nation’s search:

  • Wales: check a registered waste carrier with the links from GOV.UK’s household waste page (Natural Resources Wales).
  • Scotland: Scottish Environment Protection Agency (SEPA), also linked from that GOV.UK page.
  • Northern Ireland: a separate register, again linked from GOV.UK.
