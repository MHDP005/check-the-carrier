Skip permits: when you need one, who applies, and what each council charges

If your skip will sit on the road, the pavement or a grass verge, the council has to say yes first. Here is how the rules work under the Highways Act 1980, and what Slough, Windsor and Maidenhead, Surrey (for Staines and Spelthorne), Hillingdon and Buckinghamshire publish about fees, timescales and conditions.

Fees, timescales and conditions on this page are what each council or GOV.UK published when we checked on 25 November 2026 (Europe/London). Councils usually change their charges in April, and some add costs for parking bay suspensions or traffic management. Check the council page, and ask your skip firm what it will add to your quote, before you book.

The short answer

  • On your drive or other private land: no permit. GOV.UK says you do not need a skip licence if the skip is entirely on private land, and Surrey County Council gives a front garden as an example.
  • Any part on the highway: you need permission from the highway authority, which is your council (in Surrey, the county council). That includes the road, the pavement and the verge. Section 139 of the Highways Act 1980 says a builder’s skip “shall not be deposited on a highway without the permission of the highway authority”.
  • Who applies: in every council area on this page, the skip company applies, and only firms registered with that council can. GOV.UK notes that in some parts of England you may have to apply yourself, so check if you are elsewhere.
  • Skip bags count too. GOV.UK’s skip licence page says you need a licence for “hippobags and other skip bags” on a public road, not just steel skips.

Council by council: the details that catch people out

Slough

Slough’s Streetworks team issues permits for skips on the carriageway, footway or verge, and only to skip hire companies registered with the council, which the council says is to make sure they have the correct insurance and follow its safety rules. The council puts the onus on you: “if you hire a skip, it is your responsibility to make sure it has been authorised.” It invites residents to email streetworks@slough.gov.uk to check that a skip they have hired is authorised, or to ask about registered companies. Because no fee is published on the permit page, ask your skip firm for the permit charge in writing as part of your quote.

Windsor and Maidenhead (RBWM)

RBWM’s licence is priced by the week, so a four-week job costs £250 in licence fees alone at April 2026 rates. Two details matter for householders. First, the firm has to apply at least 10 working days ahead, so a same-week booking for a road skip may not be possible. Second, RBWM says it can fine £250 for each breach of the licence conditions, and an unlicensed skip on the highway carries a £650 fine. If an application is withdrawn, the council keeps a £50 admin charge. The council also caps licensed containers at 5m long and 2m wide, and asks for a site location plan, so tell the firm exactly where you want the skip to go.

Surrey (Staines and Spelthorne)

Surrey’s fee is a flat £139 for up to 28 days, and it aims to issue a licence within 2 working days of a full application. Surrey says skips should be placed within property boundaries “where practicable”. Its published conditions are some of the most detailed locally:

  • The skip must be clearly visible from at least 100m in both directions, and not within 30m of a road junction, or blocking sight lines, drains, fire hydrants, manholes or access to property.
  • In the road, it must sit near the edge of the carriageway with its long side parallel to the kerb, with at least three traffic cones on the approach side.
  • At night, the owner must light four yellow lamps, one on each corner, plus lamps between the cones.
  • A skip must not block a footway without the council’s express permission, which is given only after consulting the police and on condition that a 1m pedestrian walkway is provided in the road, protected and lit.
  • Contents must be damped down or covered to stop dust, and a full skip must be removed from the highway within 2 working days.
  • No flammable, explosive, putrescible (rotting), noxious or offensive waste may go in a skip on the highway.

Hillingdon

Hillingdon is clear that the permit is the skip company’s job, not yours, and that paying for an application does not mean instant permission. Where parking restrictions apply, the applicant must get any exemption from Parking Services before applying. The council lists situations that can affect permission or need a site visit, including within 50m of a school, on a road less than 5m wide, less than 15m from a junction or bend, on a main A or B road, within 15m of a width restriction, or on a high street. Once an application is under way, no refunds are given. Hillingdon also checks that registered operators hold a waste carrier’s licence and at least £5 million public liability insurance.

Buckinghamshire

Buckinghamshire’s 28-day licence is the most expensive on this page at £189.63, and the penalty for getting it wrong is steep: a retrospective licence for a skip already on the road costs £564.48. If the skip will go in a parking area or bay, a parking suspension must be applied for before the skip licence, or you “will be subject to penalty charges”. A licence can be renewed twice at £66.15 each time. After that, a new licence is needed.

What the law says about safety

Section 139 of the Highways Act 1980 lets the council attach conditions about where the skip goes, its size, its paint and markings, its contents, lighting and guarding, and its removal. Once a skip is on the highway, the skip owner must make sure it is:

  • properly lit during the hours of darkness;
  • marked as the regulations require. Under the Builders’ Skips (Markings) Regulations 1984, each end of a skip on the road (other than on a footway or verge) must carry two red fluorescent and yellow reflective plates;
  • clearly and indelibly marked with the owner’s name and phone number or address;
  • removed as soon as practicable after it is full; and
  • kept within every condition of the permit.

Breaking these rules is an offence with a fine of up to level 3 on the standard scale. GOV.UK says that failing to put up the required lights and markings could lead to a fine of up to £1,000. The law also says that where an offence is due to “the act or default of some other person”, that person can be guilty too. So if you move the cones or take away a lamp, the problem can become yours.

Even with a valid permit, section 140 lets the highway authority or a police officer in uniform require the skip to be moved or repositioned, or move it themselves and recover the cost from the skip owner.

Can a skip go on the pavement?

Usually not. GOV.UK’s skip licence page says plainly, “You can’t put a skip on the pavement.” Surrey allows it only with express permission after consulting the police, and only if a protected 1m walkway is provided in the road. If the only space outside your home is the footway, ask the skip firm whether the council will accept it before you book, or look at alternatives such as a skip bag on the drive, a man and van, or the council’s bulky waste collection. A checklist for booking a skip on the road

  1. Measure the space on your drive first. If the skip fits entirely on private land, you avoid the permit, the lights and the cones. Our skip sizes guide lists typical footprints.
  2. If it must go on the road, tell the firm when you book, and give the exact spot. RBWM asks for a site plan and Hillingdon may send an officer to look.
  3. Check the firm is registered with your council as a skip operator (Slough will confirm by email) and registered as an upper-tier waste carrier on the Environment Agency register. Our licence-checking guide shows how.
  4. Allow time. RBWM wants 10 working days’ notice, Hillingdon aims for 5 working days, and Surrey for 2. Ask about parking bay suspensions if there are restrictions outside.
  5. Get the permit charge in writing. Ask whether the quote includes the council fee for the whole hire, and what an extension costs (for example £139 per 28 days in Surrey or £66.15 in Buckinghamshire).
  6. Do not let the skip arrive before the permit. Hillingdon warns not to put a skip on the highway without a valid permit, and Buckinghamshire charges £564.48 for a retrospective licence.
  7. Keep the lamps and cones in place, and keep restricted items out. See what you can’t put in a skip.
  8. Book the collection promptly when it is full. Surrey says within 2 working days.
