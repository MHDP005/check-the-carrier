What you can’t put in a skip, and where it goes instead

Asbestos, plasterboard, fridges, tyres, batteries, paint, gas bottles, mattresses and old sofas all cause trouble in a general skip. Here is why each one is restricted, and the legal route for it, including the local rules at Chalvey (Slough) and Stafferton Way (Windsor and Maidenhead).

  • 9 restricted items
  • Local tip rules
  • Checked 23 Nov 2026

Quick answer

Keep asbestos, plasterboard, fridges and electricals, tyres, batteries, paint and chemicals, gas bottles, mattresses and upholstered sofas out of a general skip. Each one has its own legal route, often through your council tip or a council collection.

Key facts

  • Asbestos: never break it up. Use an HSE-licensed contractor for anything beyond small amounts of bonded cement sheet.
  • Plasterboard at Chalvey (Slough) is charged as DIY waste: £9 a sheet up to 2m × 1m.
  • Mattresses at Chalvey: up to two a month, then £24 each.
  • Tyres at Chalvey: £5 each, up to five a year.
  • Paint and chemicals at Chalvey: up to 5 litres a visit, one visit a month.

Watch out

Rules, limits and charges on this page are what each council, government body or firm published when we checked on 23 November 2026 (Europe/London). Site rules change, sometimes at short notice. Always check the council page before you drive to a recycling centre, and tell a skip firm exactly what you plan to load when you book.

The short answer

Every skip firm has its own list, but the same items come up again and again. Allwaste (Berkshire) says it excludes asbestos, fridges, freezers, batteries and tyres.

FJL Waste Services says asbestos, paint, solvents, oils and other liquids, batteries, electricals and vapes, fluorescent tubes, gas bottles and tyres cannot go in, and that plasterboard must be kept separate.

HIPPO’s accepted waste guide marks mattresses, sofas, upholstered chairs, fridges and freezers, tyres and plasterboard as not accepted in its skips.

FJL warns that “a contaminated load is usually rejected and charged for”, and HIPPO says you will be liable for any additional charges if restricted items are found.

Asbestos

The Health and Safety Executive says asbestos was widely used in homes until 1999, when it was banned. Its advice to householders is clear: do not try to repair or remove asbestos yourself without training, and if it is in good condition it is often best left where it is.

Show the full detail

Some materials, such as sprayed coatings, lagging and asbestos insulating board, should only be removed by a contractor licensed by HSE. HSE also says asbestos must be disposed of as hazardous waste and not mixed with normal household waste. That is why no general skip will take it.

HSE’s packaging standard is a red inner bag with asbestos warning labels inside a clear outer bag. Intact cement sheets should not be broken up. Wrap them in 1000-gauge polythene sheeting instead, and label them.

  • Slough: Slough Borough Council accepts bonded (cement) asbestos from Slough homes through the Chalvey Waste Transfer Station, for residents with proof of address and photo ID. You can bring no more than five small sheets that fit in a car. Two 1m × 1m sheets are free, then £4.80 per 1m × 1m piece. The asbestos must be dampened and double bagged or wrapped tightly, and you must tell staff when you arrive. For anything larger, or any other type of asbestos, the council points you to an HSE-licensed asbestos removal contractor.
  • Windsor and Maidenhead: Stafferton Way accepts asbestos and Artex-coated plasterboard if it is double wrapped and labelled as asbestos waste, up to three sheets (for example 2m × 1m) or sacks of cement-bonded asbestos. Avoid breaking up large pieces.

Slough’s asbestos page also mentions Langley Household Recycling Centre (run by Buckinghamshire Council), but Slough’s recycling centre page says Langley is closed for maintenance until spring 2027.

Plasterboard

Plasterboard is made from gypsum. Environment Agency guidance says gypsum-based waste must not be sent to a landfill cell that accepts biodegradable waste, and must be separated for reuse or recovery. Mixed waste is harder to treat, so skip firms ask you to keep plasterboard out of the general load.

Show the full detail

FJL says to keep it separate and mention it when you book. HIPPO accepts clean plasterboard only if it is in its own bag, and says: “Due to EA legislation, plasterboard cannot be accepted if mixed with other waste streams.”

  • Chalvey (Slough): plasterboard is charged as DIY waste: £9 per sheet up to 2m × 1m, or £7.60 per 25-litre bag of plaster or gypsum. The plasterboard skip is in the Waste Transfer Station, and tiles, wood and other materials must be removed first.
  • Stafferton Way (RBWM): plasterboard is on the list of items accepted but not recycled. Home improvement waste is limited to one car boot load (six sacks) per trip, per month.

Fridges, freezers and other electricals

Skip firms such as Allwaste and HIPPO exclude fridges and freezers, and FJL excludes electricals generally. The legal routes are straightforward:

  • The shop. GOV.UK says that when a retailer sells you a new electrical item, it must give you a way to dispose of the old one of the same type, either free in store or through another free take-back service. GOV.UK’s household waste page gives the example of handing in your old kettle when you buy a new one.
  • Recycling centres. Chalvey accepts fridges, freezers, large and small appliances, TVs, monitors and all household battery types, plus up to four fluorescent tubes a year. Stafferton Way accepts large and small electricals, screens, bulbs, tubes and vapes, but lists lithium fridges as excluded. Empty food out of appliances first.
  • Council collection. Slough collects up to two fridges or freezers at £29.70 each, with a £54 minimum charge. RBWM charges £55 per fridge or freezer, but does not collect American-style or commercial units.

Tyres

Environment Agency landfill guidance lists whole or shredded used tyres as banned from landfill, apart from bicycle tyres and tyres over 1,400mm in diameter. Skip firms therefore exclude them. Locally:

  • Chalvey takes car and motorcycle tyres at £5 each, up to five a year. One Slough page says the charge applies after five free tyres, and another lists £5 per tyre with tyres over the yearly limit refused, so check on arrival. Chalvey is open to both Slough and RBWM residents.
  • Stafferton Way lists car tyres as excluded, and neither council’s bulky collection takes tyres.
  • Otherwise use a registered carrier that says it takes tyres. HIPPO, for example, lists tyres as accepted by its man and van service for an extra fee. Check the carrier on the register first.

Batteries

HIPPO lists batteries as not accepted in any of its services and explains that some waste presents a serious risk of fire and explosion to staff, vehicles and waste sites. FJL lists vapes alongside batteries, so take those out of the skip too.

Chalvey accepts all household battery types and up to two car batteries a year. Stafferton Way accepts household batteries for recycling and car batteries for disposal. Neither council’s large item collection takes car batteries.

Paint, solvents, oils and chemicals

The landfill rules ban liquid waste and waste that would be explosive, corrosive, oxidising or flammable. FJL lists paint, solvents, oils and other liquids as needing separate disposal. HIPPO accepts paint tins only when the lids are off and the tins are dry with no residue.

Show the full detail
  • Chalvey takes paint, thinners, wood treatments, garden chemicals, cleaners and car fluids if they are in their original or clearly marked containers. The limit is 5 litres of household and garden chemicals per visit, with one visit a month. Tell staff when you arrive, and they will unlock the hazardous chemical store for you. Engine oil is limited to 10 litres a year. Petrol and other flammable liquids are not accepted at all.
  • Stafferton Way accepts paint and motor oil for recycling and chemicals for disposal, but also lists “hazardous waste” and “oils” as excluded. If you are unsure, check with the council before you go.

Gas bottles and cylinders

RBWM says gas cylinders should never go in rubbish or recycling bins because of the high risk of fire if a cylinder is crushed or punctured. That applies to skips too, and FJL and HIPPO exclude them. RBWM says to approach the supplier of the cylinder first.

Stafferton Way and Chalvey both accept gas bottles, including helium and fast gas canisters at Stafferton Way, but you must hand them to a member of staff. Neither council’s bulky collection takes gas canisters.

Mattresses

Mattresses are not hazardous, and GOV.UK’s POPs guidance says they are not classed as domestic seating. They are still bulky and costly to process, and HIPPO says it does not accept them in skips. It takes them in a skip bag or man and van collection for an extra charge. Your options:

Show the full detail
  • Chalvey: up to two mattresses, one visit per month, then £24 per mattress.
  • Stafferton Way: mattresses accepted (not recycled).
  • Council collection: Slough charges £19 an item (a bed base and mattress count as two items) with a £54 minimum. RBWM charges from £50 for one item to £80 for five.

Sofas, armchairs and other upholstered seating (POPs waste)

This is the rule that surprises most people. GOV.UK guidance says waste upholstered domestic seating may contain persistent organic pollutants (POPs), usually flame retardants in the covers and foam.

It covers sofas, sofa beds, armchairs, recliners, upholstered kitchen and dining chairs, garden furniture with cushions, stools and footstools, futons, bean bags, floor and sofa cushions, and items such as child car seats and highchairs.

Show the full detail

Unupholstered wooden chairs and deckchairs are unlikely to contain POPs.

The guidance says this waste must be kept separate from other waste, “in a skip or container”. If it is mixed into a skip, the whole load must be described as containing POPs. It must be destroyed in an authorised incinerator or cement kiln, and landfill is not allowed.

That is why skip firms, including HIPPO, will not take upholstered furniture in a skip or bag.

  • Chalvey: Slough says it is temporarily unable to accept items containing POPs, and points residents to Burnham Household Recycling Centre (Buckinghamshire), or to Stafferton Way for RBWM residents.
  • Stafferton Way accepts bulky items such as furniture.
  • Council collection: both Slough and RBWM collect sofas and armchairs. A three-piece suite counts as three items with both councils.
  • A clearance firm: a man and van service that collects sofas separately (HIPPO lists them for an extra fee) is another option. Check the firm is registered as an upper-tier carrier first.

Before you take anything to the tip

  • Chalvey HWRC, White Hart Road, off Spackmans Way, SL1 2SF, is for Slough and RBWM residents. Bring proof of address and photo ID. The height barrier is 1.7m, and vans and pickups are not allowed (they must use the Waste Transfer Station). Winter hours (1 October to 31 March) are 9am to 5pm daily, with last entry 15 minutes before closing. Soil is not accepted. DIY waste is free up to four times in four weeks, within set amounts, and charged by card above that.
  • Stafferton Way HWRC, Stafferton Way, Maidenhead SL6 1AY, is for RBWM residents only. Bring your Advantage Card, or photo ID with proof of address. The height barrier is 2.1m, and you need a van permit for every visit in a van. From October to March it opens at 8am with last entry at 4.15pm. Loft insulation, Celotex, uPVC windows and door frames, and other construction and demolition waste are excluded.

A quick checklist before your skip arrives

  1. Walk round the job and set aside anything on this page: upholstered seating, mattresses, electricals, batteries, tyres, tins and bottles of liquid, gas cylinders and plasterboard.
  2. If you suspect asbestos, stop. Do not break it up. Read HSE’s advice, and use an HSE-licensed contractor for anything beyond small amounts of bonded cement sheet.
  3. Tell the skip firm about anything you are unsure of when you book. Some firms, FJL for example, say they can arrange separate collection for hazardous items.
  4. Book the council collection or plan a tip run for the restricted items, keeping within each site’s limits.
  5. Check every carrier on the Environment Agency register, upper tier, before anything leaves your home. GOV.UK says you could be fined if your waste goes to someone not allowed to take it. Our licence-checking guide shows how.
