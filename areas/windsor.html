If the skip will sit on a Windsor road

Windsor is in the Royal Borough of Windsor and Maidenhead (RBWM).

A skip placed entirely on your own drive or private land does not need a council licence, but one on the public highway does. RBWM’s skip licence page says applications can only be made by skip companies registered with the council, and should go in at least 10 working days before the skip is needed.

The skip must not be more than 5m long or 2m wide.

The council’s listed fees (as at 1 April 2026) are £90 for the admin fee and first week, £130 for two weeks, £190 for three weeks and £250 for four weeks, and £90 for a single wait and load of 30 minutes to 2 hours.

Show the full detail

The council says it is an offence to place a skip on the highway without a licence, and that an unlicensed skip carries a fine of £650. Most skip firms apply for the licence and add the cost to your quote, so ask whether it is included.

Council options first

For a few bulky items, the RBWM large item collection may be enough. When we checked on 20 November 2026 it cost £50 for one item, £57 for two, £65 for three, £72 for four and £80 for five (the maximum), or £55 per fridge or freezer, with a reduction for people receiving housing benefit.

The council will not collect building or DIY waste, garden waste, rubble, soil, glass or mirrors, among other items.

Royal Borough residents can also take household waste to Stafferton Way Household Waste and Recycling Centre, Stafferton Way, Maidenhead SL6 1AY. Bring your Advantage Card, or photo ID with proof of address.

Show the full detail

It is vehicle-only with a 2.1m height barrier, opens at 8am every day except Christmas Day, Boxing Day and New Year’s Day, and last entry is 4.15pm from October to March. If you are going in a van you need a van permit for every visit.

The council also says that waste you have paid someone to take away counts as commercial waste and will not be accepted there.
