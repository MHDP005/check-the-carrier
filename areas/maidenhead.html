Can you just take it to Stafferton Way?

Maidenhead has the Royal Borough’s own tip on its doorstep: Stafferton Way Household Waste and Recycling Centre, Stafferton Way, SL6 1AY (follow signs for the Civic Amenity Centre). For many clear-outs a few car trips there is cheaper than any skip.

The catch is that the rules are tighter than many people expect, especially for renovation waste. This is what the council’s pages said when we checked on 22 November 2026:

What Stafferton Way HWRC accepts and the limits that apply, according to RBWM

Who can use it

Royal Borough residents only. Bring your Advantage Card, or photo ID with proof of address such as a council tax bill, and display it on the dashboard.

Your own DIY or home improvement waste

The equivalent of one car boot load (six sacks) per trip, per month. Hardcore and soil, wood and plasterboard are on the accepted lists.

Never accepted

Demolition and construction waste including Celotex or other insulation, loft insulation, uPVC window and door frames, car tyres, clinical and hazardous waste, ink and toner cartridges, among others. Anyone bringing these will be asked to take them away.

Asbestos

No more than three sheets (for example 2m × 1m) or sacks of cement-bonded asbestos, double wrapped and labelled as asbestos waste.

Going in a van

You need a van permit for every visit. Vans over 5.5m long, flatbed or cage tippers, long wheelbase panel vans, rear twin-axle vehicles and Luton vans are not accepted. There is a 2.1m height barrier.

Trailers

Accepted without a van permit, up to 2m × 1.3m.

Rubbish someone else removes

If you pay someone to take your rubbish, it becomes commercial waste and will not be accepted at the site. That is why a clearance firm must have its own licensed route for disposal, and why you should check its registration.

Opening times

Open seven days a week from 8am, closed only on Christmas Day, Boxing Day and New Year’s Day. Last entry 7.45pm from April to September and 4.15pm from October to March.

Show the full detail

So the decision is usually simple. If you are stripping out a kitchen, bathroom or extension, or the waste includes insulation or old windows, you will need a skip, a skip bag or a licensed clearance firm.

If it is a garage of household clutter, a car and a few weekends at Stafferton Way may do the job.

For a handful of bulky items the council’s large item collection starts at £50 for one item and £80 for five (fees as at 1 April 2026), but it will not take building or DIY waste, including kitchen cupboards, sinks, radiators, doors or bathroom furniture.

Chalvey HWRC in Slough is the council’s listed alternative site.

If the skip has to go on the road

If there is no room on your drive, the skip will have to go on the road. A skip on the public highway needs an RBWM licence, and only skip companies registered with the council can apply, at least 10 working days before the skip is needed.

The fees and size limits are the same across the borough; see our Windsor page for the full fee list. Two details matter for you as the customer.

If the firm breaks the licence conditions, the council says it can fine £250 for each contravention, and a skip on the highway without any licence carries a £650 fine.

Some firms on this page, such as FJL, say they only place skips on private land, so tell them first if your skip would have to go on the street. Others, such as HIPPO, say they arrange the permit.
