Quick answer

Uxbridge is all Hillingdon Council: bins, bulky collections, the Harefield tip and skip permits. If your skip goes on the road, the skip firm applies for the permit (£90 for residents), not you.

Key facts

  • 5 firms listed, each with a live upper-tier registration when we checked on 26 November 2026.
  • Hillingdon skip permit: £90 for residents, £94.50 for non-residents. The council aims to decide in 5 working days.
  • Harefield tip: DIY waste is free up to 5 black bags or 4 single items a day. Above that, £130 a tonne for recycled loads and £333 for mixed.
  • Save money: keep plasterboard out of your rubble, or the whole load is charged as mixed waste.
  • Bulky items: Hillingdon collects up to 6 items for £53 (£39 for over-65s).

One council for everything, except just over the river

Uxbridge is in the London Borough of Hillingdon, which is simpler than our Staines page, where the job is split between two councils. Hillingdon Council empties your bins, runs the bulky waste collection, runs the tip at Harefield and grants permits for skips on the road.

Uxbridge does sit right on the county boundary, though. Denham, Iver and Gerrards Cross, which several of the firms below also cover, are in Buckinghamshire, so a skip on the road there needs a Buckinghamshire Council licence instead. Our skip permits guide compares both.

Could you take it to Harefield tip instead?

Hillingdon’s main tip is Harefield Civic Amenity Site, New Years Green Lane, Harefield, UB9 6LX, north of Uxbridge. It takes rubble, soil, plasterboard, kitchen units, bathroom suites, doors and timber, but home improvement waste counts as commercial waste, and only a small amount is free.

This is what Hillingdon Council’s pages said when we checked on 26 November 2026:

Harefield Civic Amenity Site rules, DIY allowance and charges, according to Hillingdon Council

Household rubbish and recycling

Free for Hillingdon residents with a HillingdonFirst card, or a driving licence and a household bill. Without proof of address, or if you live outside Hillingdon and come by car, it costs £11 a visit. The site is for vehicles only; pedestrians cannot carry waste in.

DIY and building waste (tiles, plasterboard, rubble, soil, kitchen units, bathroom suites, doors, fencing, sheds)

Free up to the equivalent of 5 black bags or 4 single items per day, such as a door, pallet, sink or fence panel. Above that, it is weighed and charged.

Charges above the allowance

Recycled loads: £130 a tonne, minimum £26 for up to 200kg. Mixed loads: £333 a tonne, minimum £33.30 for up to 100kg. If DIY waste is mixed with household rubbish, everything is weighed at trade prices, because the site cannot separate it.

Going in a van

Bring two forms of ID: photo ID and a household bill dated within the last three months (no driving licence means a passport and two proofs of address).

Resident vans are limited to 12 visits a year, January to December, and a van with ID in someone else’s name may be turned away.

Asbestos

Hillingdon offers residents a free asbestos collection of up to 6 sheets or bags in a rolling 12 months, each bag no more than 10kg. Extra bags or sheets are £14.40 each including VAT. Asbestos cannot go in any skip.

Fridges, freezers and gas cylinders

Fridges, freezers and other cooling appliances are temporarily not accepted at any Hillingdon site because of a national shortage of recycling capacity. Gas cylinders, fire extinguishers and similar are never accepted.

Rubbish someone else removes

Anyone bringing business waste needs a valid waste carrier licence and a completed waste transfer note, or the site may refuse it. Hillingdon warns residents to use only a licensed carrier and to ask where their waste is going.

Opening times

8am to 4pm every day. Do not arrive before 7.45am; last entry for residents is 3.45pm. Closed Christmas Day, Boxing Day and New Year’s Day.

At the busiest times queues average around an hour and a half; the council says Wednesdays and Thursdays tend to be quieter, and to avoid 11am to midday.

The split between “recycled” and “mixed” matters a lot. A tonne of clean hardcore and soil counts as a recycled load at £130, but a recycled load must contain only one stream, and hardcore must have no plasterboard in it.

Throw the plasterboard in with the rubble and the same tonne is charged as mixed waste at £333. If you are doing a bathroom or a small extension yourself, keeping rubble, plasterboard and wood apart is the cheapest way to use Harefield.

If the job will produce several tonnes, or you have no van, compare the charges with the skip prices below, remembering that the tip figures do not include getting the waste there.

Highway works on New Years Green Lane started on 5 October 2026 and were expected to last about ten weeks; the site stays open but the council warns of delays. It also asks drivers not to turn right into New Years Green Lane from Harvil Road, but to join the queue coming down from Harefield.

There is a reuse centre on site for good furniture and household items.

Changes this winter. Hillingdon’s weekend-only West Drayton waste site on Tavistock Road closes after its last weekend of 19 and 20 December 2026.

Instead, Hillingdon residents can use Space Waye Reuse and Recycling Centre, run with Hounslow Council at North Feltham Trading Estate, Pier Road, Feltham TW14 0TH.

It is open 8am to 3.30pm every day, with no booking needed Monday to Thursday, but a booked slot is needed on Fridays, weekends and bank holidays. Bring proof of your Hillingdon address.

Furniture and garden waste without a skip

For a few large household items, Hillingdon’s bulky waste collection costs £53 for residents, £39 for over-65s and £94 for landlords, for one collection of up to 6 items, such as beds, mattresses, sofas, wardrobes and washing machines. Three black bags of smaller items can replace one item.

You book it through the council’s Love Clean Streets website or app, and the items must be out at the front of your property before 6am.

It will not take building or DIY waste, including bathroom furniture, kitchen cupboards, sinks, radiators and doors, or garden waste, soil, rubble, tiles or sheds, and fridge and freezer collections have been suspended since August.

For a garden clear-out spread over time, Hillingdon’s garden waste subscription costs £77 for 2026/27 (1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027), with three sacks and tags as standard, collected weekly from March to November and fortnightly in winter. For a single big clearance, Harefield takes garden waste, or a skip will.

If the skip has to go on the road

Many Uxbridge terraces and flats have no drive, so the skip has to go on the road. Hillingdon Council’s skip permit page says the permit is the skip company’s responsibility, not the householder’s or the builder’s. What the council publishes:

  • The fee is £90 per application for residents and £94.50 for non-residents, paid online, and no refund is given once processing starts.
  • The council aims to decide a new permit in 5 working days, or longer if a highways officer needs to visit, and an extension in 3 working days. Tacit consent does not apply, so no answer means no permit.
  • Where there are parking restrictions, the applicant must get an exemption from Parking Services first.
  • Permission can be affected if the spot is within 50 metres of a school, on a road under five metres wide, less than 15 metres from a junction or bend, on a main A or B road, within 15 metres of a width restriction, or on a high street. In Uxbridge that can include spots on the High Street and the main A roads through the town.
  • A licensed skip must have lights on each corner after dark, yellow ends, the owner’s name and address or phone number, must not block drains or access, and must not sit on a verge unless the council has authorised it.
Show the full detail

Hillingdon only grants permits to skip operators it has registered, and it checks for a waste carrier’s licence, an operator’s licence and at least £5 million of public liability insurance.

The council does link to a “skip operating companies registered with the council” list, but the PDF it serves is dated 2011, so do not treat it as current: ask your firm directly whether it is registered with Hillingdon.

You can report a skip that looks unlicensed or unsafe through Love Clean Streets.

On this page, B&K and Quick Skip Hire say they arrange the permit, Sunrise and Lita say they can help, and FJL says it only places skips on private land.
